# Food logging and goal semantics

## Product boundary

Food replaces Challenges in the five-item navigation. `/challenges`, its provider, and existing challenge records remain intact but have no normal product link or Today preview. Authenticated users see only persisted food, check-ins, and XP. The explicit `DUOPET_DATA_MODE=demo` switch remains confined to the existing data-source boundary; there are no food fixtures.

## Catalog and diary

`foods` is a duo-shared catalog. Either member can search and log an active food; only its creator can edit or archive the definition. Nutrition values come from the user, not an external API. A catalog edit changes future logs only. `food_log_entries` is a personally owned diary: `user_id` is always the authenticated caller, and each row stores the food name, serving text, calories, and protein at logging time. Changing quantity retains these snapshots. Deleting or changing a row is limited to the current duo-local day. Archived definitions disappear from search but their old diary rows remain readable and editable by their owner on the same day. The partner may read the diary only while its owner shares it.

The Food page loads the current user's diary and at most 200 rows from the last 30 days for suggestions. Recent foods are unique foods sorted by last use; frequent foods are sorted by use count in that bounded window. Search uses case-insensitive `ILIKE`, a 25-row limit, and a duo/name index. No nutrition values or history are fabricated. Calories display as whole kcal, protein to one decimal gram; stored values remain numeric snapshots. You/Partner and Today/History controls expose 14 recent duo-local dates. Selecting a date queries only that date's saved entries (at most 100 rows); displayed nutrition always comes from historical snapshots.

## Nutrition → canonical goals

Goals now have `progress_source` (`manual`, `food_calories`, `food_protein`) and `target_direction` (`minimum`, `maximum`, `range`). Food RPCs run as a transaction: write the caller's diary row, sum that caller's per-day snapshot × quantity, then upsert their food-sourced goal check-ins. A duo row lock serializes concurrent diary writes and day finalization. Both members can read aggregate goal check-ins through the existing goal policies; neither can read the other's diary. Direct authenticated writes to `goal_checkins` have been revoked. `set_goal_checkin` rejects food-sourced goals. The food-sync helper is private and uncallable by browser roles.

Protein is a minimum goal: it completes at or above the user's own target snapshot, awards +10 XP once, and reverses that event if nutrition falls below target. Study and boolean goals are unchanged. Manual maximum goals remain supported, with their existing day-close semantics.

### Calories: required input and a dynamic allowance

Calories remains a food-derived `range` definition for schema compatibility, extended with **`logged_required`** semantics. For future/open days there is no lower success bound and no partial XP tier. Given that day's personal target T:

| Final food-derived total | Outcome | Completed | Goal XP |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No real food entries | Unlogged | No | 0 |
| At least one food entry and total ≤ T + 200 | Within allowance | Yes, after day close | 10 |
| At least one food entry and total > T + 200 | Over allowance | No | 0 |

The upper boundary is inclusive. At target 1700, genuinely logged 900, 1300, 1500, 1700, and 1900 kcal all earn the same 10 XP; 1901 fails. At target 2000, 2200 succeeds and 2201 fails. Decimal totals are compared without display rounding. A real zero-nutrition food is still input; an automatically created zero-valued check-in is not. Each member uses their own versioned target snapshot. Eating less earns no additional reward.

Today, Tasks, and Food show consumed, target, allowed upper limit, and neutral “Currently within allowance” / “over allowance” copy. No entries shows “No food logged.” Neither an under-limit total nor input alone awards calorie XP before day close. Historical task/food views distinguish unlogged, completed, and over-limit days. Habit performance reports completed, failed, and unlogged days; partial-credit counts are explicitly legacy-only. Completion percentage is not an XP percentage.

### Trusted close and immutable history

The SQL evaluator `private.goal_completion_tier` drives stored `completion_tier` and `completed`; the XP trigger uses the existing deterministic event key. Required-input days yield `unlogged`, `pending`, `full`, or `failed`. Full earns 10. Unlogged/over-limit days block the shared-completion bonus and Perfect Duo Day. Genuinely logged low intake counts as completed after close. Open calorie days block Perfect Duo Day until finalization. Clients cannot submit input evidence, tiers, tolerance snapshots, completion results, finalization timestamps, food-derived values, or XP awards.

`finalize_due_goal_days()` closes maximum **and range** days through yesterday, under the existing duo lock, using the duo's stored IANA timezone and historical assignments/status. A missing projection reads authoritative diary snapshot × quantity totals. Its snapshot trigger also derives `has_food_log` from real diary rows; a no-input day becomes `unlogged`, not successful zero intake. Retry skips finalized check-ins and cannot duplicate XP. Finalized snapshots cannot change value, target, direction, tolerances, input evidence, rule, or close timestamp.

The existing hourly Cron job `duopet-finalize-due-days` (`17 * * * *`) still calls `private.run_scheduled_finalization()`. That wrapper invokes the same goal finalizer and challenge finalizer. No new job or parallel implementation is introduced. Authenticated load/day-rollover remains a safe fallback.

Check-ins save `direction_snapshot`, `target_snapshot`, `unit_snapshot`, `logged_required_snapshot`, authoritative `has_food_log`, and tolerances. Required-input days use only `upper_tolerance_snapshot`; lower/partial columns remain solely for compatibility. Today target changes deliberately change only today's target snapshot; Tomorrow edits leave it intact. Future target/rule changes cannot reinterpret finalized snapshots. Domain adapters and realtime merges preserve the day's semantics.

### Activity streaks, independently of Perfect Duo Days

The main/current/best streak is the caller's **activity streak**. `user_activity_days` records one server-derived row per duo/user/local date after a qualifying action: checking a boolean goal, recording positive manual measured progress, or adding/editing a food log. Merely opening the app, partner actions, automatic food projections, passive zero rows, XP events, and finalization do not record activity. Triggers require `auth.uid()` to match the actor and the date to equal the duo's current local date. Browser roles cannot insert/update/delete activity days or submit streak counts.

The first input counts immediately; consecutive active dates form runs. Current streak includes today when active; otherwise it can continue through yesterday. Once a full day is missed it becomes zero. Best streak is the longest lifetime activity run. Undo/deleting an entry does not erase the fact that the user interacted that day, but deleting the last food entry restores Calories to unlogged. Backfill uses only retained positive manual check-ins and real diary entries; previously deleted/undone input cannot be reconstructed and is not fabricated.

`get_pet_stats` derives caller-specific runs from these rows while XP and Perfect Duo Day counts remain duo-owned. Today, Progress, Profile, and Pet all consume this same result. Progress's 14-day activity dots query the same table; Perfect Duo Day rings and counts remain separate stricter achievements. The existing runtime subscription listens for the current user's new activity day and mutations also immediately refresh authoritative runtime state. RLS permits only self activity-day reads; there is currently no partner-streak display.

### Range migration / legacy days

**Current correction:** `20260929155935_activity_streak_and_logged_calories.sql` adds activity days and required-input snapshots. Food-calorie definitions record `logged_rule_effective_from` in the duo timezone. Today's open check-ins adopt the rule and recompute authoritative totals/input evidence; future inserts snapshot it. Previously finalized days and their XP events are untouched, including legacy +5 partial awards. Older closed-but-unfinalized projections keep their saved legacy semantics; missing pre-transition projections use legacy rules. The evaluator delegates those days to the unchanged legacy function. No legacy days are fabricated as new-rule successes or rewritten to remove rewards. No Cron job is added or removed.

`20260928141415_calorie_target_range.sql` converts food-calorie definitions from maximum to range in place, preserving IDs, targets/versions, lifecycle, food logs, check-ins, XP, and dormant challenge links. The exact old starter note is updated to target-range copy; custom notes are preserved. Fresh starter seeding uses range immediately. No existing check-in is updated or recreated, and no prior XP is recalculated.

Each converted definition records `range_effective_from` in its duo timezone. New check-ins from that date use range snapshots. An existing check-in, including an already-open maximum day, keeps its saved maximum semantics and original full 10-XP eligibility. A missing older projection finalized later uses legacy maximum semantics. Historical minimum/manual calorie check-ins also retain their original semantics. Legacy days are never presented as fabricated range outcomes. This explicit transition means an already-logged production day may finish under legacy rules; the next new day uses range.

## Starter migration

The additive migration leaves all existing users, duos, food-independent goals, check-ins, XP ledger rows, and challenge records in place. Existing shared manual Diet/Calories (`calories`, `kcal`) and Protein/Protein Intake (`protein`, `g`) definitions are archived and replaced by food-linked definitions with each member's current target. The archived originals preserve old manual history and challenge links. An untouched starter Diet is renamed Calories in its replacement; customized Diet copy remains Diet. Paused goals remain paused. The conversion is selected by name, icon, unit, and tracking shape rather than by loose goal-name matching at runtime. Fresh duo seeding creates Calories and Protein Intake as food-sourced goals from the start. 8K Steps remains boolean. Running the conversion block again would skip the archived originals and the new non-manual definitions. Today's pre-migration manual check-ins remain historical records on archived originals; existing XP for those check-ins is not erased. A previously awarded *open-day* perfect-day event is reversed if a new calorie maximum makes that day no longer final.

The follow-up migration `202609250002_preserve_food_target_schedules.sql` copies future target versions only when one archived manual goal uniquely matches one new food goal from the same conversion moment. It does not overwrite a newer scheduled target the user already chose on the replacement. Custom goals with a different name/icon shape are deliberately left manual. This avoids silently converting a user's unrelated measured goal. A future opt-in conversion flow could cover those cases while retaining the old definition.

## Partner sharing and privacy

`sharing_preferences` stores three independent flags for each user. Existing and new users default to ON for personal goals, food diary, and nutrition totals. The row is created when its profile is created. Each member can read their partner's flags but update only their own. Profile fields remain self-only. The Profile page saves each switch immediately, disables it while saving, and reports success or failure.

Personal goals are read-only to the partner when `share_personal_goals` is ON; the owner alone can edit, pause, archive, change targets, and check in. When OFF, goal definitions, assignments, check-ins, and status events are filtered by RLS. Shared goals remain visible. Today and Tasks label partner personal goals and omit edit controls. The dormant challenge score RPC also filters private personal-goal scores.

Diary sharing governs individual food entries, servings, macros, timestamps, and history. Nutrition sharing independently governs the secure `get_food_totals` aggregate RPC and partner reads of food-derived goal check-ins. Diary OFF/totals ON shows aggregate totals without entries. Both OFF shows a private state. Diary ON/totals OFF still exposes the per-entry calories and protein specified for shared diary entries; a viewer can calculate a sum themselves. Thus totals OFF cannot make the mathematical total unknowable while detailed diary sharing remains ON. The UI suppresses aggregate cards, Today goal values, and per-goal Progress rates, while the database denies the aggregate RPC/check-ins. Historical overall percentages omit the protected partner food assignments instead of treating them as failed days. Users who want nutrition amounts fully private must turn both settings OFF.

`food_day_updates` contains only user/day/timestamp and is written by a trigger after log changes. Partner SELECT requires either diary or nutrition sharing; no browser write grant exists. Its single user-scoped realtime subscription invalidates the displayed day even when diary details are private but totals are shared. A sharing-preference change immediately clears cached partner goal state and runtime history/activity before an authoritative refetch. Food history clears entries and totals on preference changes, and late responses from old queries are ignored. RLS remains the final read boundary; hiding alone is never trusted.

## Security and realtime

RLS restricts catalog reads/writes to the duo and definition edits to the creator. Diary RLS permits self reads and partner reads only with owner consent; browser roles have no direct diary write grants. `save_food_log` and `delete_food_log` use `auth.uid()`, verify membership and ownership, and cannot write another user's diary. Manual goal writes use the guarded RPC. Clients cannot set `finalized_at`, XP events, or a food-derived check-in. The legacy assignment/definition replacement RPC execute grants are revoked; own-target editing continues through `set_my_goal_target`.

The Food page has a duo-scoped channel for catalog/current-user diary changes and a user-scoped day signal while viewing history or the partner. Subscriptions clean up on unmount. `GoalProvider` subscribes to canonical check-in changes for both members, so authorized partner nutrition aggregates, Today, XP, and Brownie update through existing paths. Realtime changes never grant data beyond RLS.
